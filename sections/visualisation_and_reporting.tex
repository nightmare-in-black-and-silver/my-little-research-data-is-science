\section{Visualisation and Reporting}

\subsection{Planned Tables and Figures}

Tables and figures will present the descriptive characteristics of the survey respondents and important patterns within the dataset. Planned tables will include demographic characteristics, fandom history and engagement, Fimfiction usage, content and character preferences, community participation, writing activity, and use of generative artificial intelligence. Cross-tabulations may be used to present subgroup and intersectional findings.

Figures may include conventional statistical visualisations and infographic-style presentations. My Little Pony-themed illustrations and visual elements may be incorporated alongside statistical information, with underlying estimates remaining clearly identifiable and documented.

\subsection{Reporting of Descriptive Estimates}

Descriptive estimates will generally be presented as frequencies and percentages for categorical and binary variables and as appropriate measures of central tendency, dispersion, and distribution for numerical and ordinal variables. Relevant sample sizes or denominators will be reported where necessary for interpretation.

Where subgroup or intersectional results are presented, the relevant subgroup denominator will be identified. Select-all-that-apply questions will indicate that categories are not mutually exclusive and that percentages may therefore exceed 100\%.

Results will distinguish characteristics of the observed survey sample from claims about the broader Fimfiction.net population.

\subsection{Rounding and Precision Conventions}

Numerical results will be reported with a level of precision appropriate to the underlying data and sample size. Percentages will use a consistent level of precision, while counts will be reported as whole numbers. Excessive numerical precision will be avoided where it does not convey meaningful information.

\subsection{Reporting of Denominators and Sample Sizes}

The overall number of eligible respondents will be reported, together with the number of responses retained following data cleaning and participant exclusions. Tables and figures will report relevant denominators where the number of valid responses differs from the overall analytical sample, particularly for questions with missing responses, conditional questions, or subgroup analyses.

For select-all-that-apply questions, percentages will ordinarily use the number of respondents providing a valid response to the question as the denominator rather than the total number of selections. For conditional questions, the denominator will reflect the population to whom the question applies.

\subsection{Treatment of Suppressed or Unstable Estimates}

Estimates based on very small subgroups will be treated cautiously because they may be imprecise, difficult to interpret, or potentially disclose information about individual respondents. Where appropriate, small categories may be combined into substantively meaningful broader categories, or estimates may be suppressed.

Small numbers alone will not constitute grounds for excluding participants.

\subsection{Consistent Presentation Conventions}

A consistent presentation framework will be used throughout the academic paper and associated visual materials. Equivalent variables and analyses will use consistent terminology, category ordering, units, rounding, denominator conventions, and statistical notation. Figures will include clear titles, labels, legends where required, and explanatory notes where interpretation could otherwise be ambiguous.

Visual and infographic material will retain the same underlying numerical estimates as the formal statistical tables and analyses. My Little Pony-themed artwork and other illustrative material will be treated as presentation elements rather than substitutes for the underlying statistical information.
